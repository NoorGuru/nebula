## The problem

A standard factory robot is a blind repeater: engineers program every motion, fence it off from people for safety, and reprogram it from scratch whenever the product changes. That makes automation rigid, integration-scarce, and off-limits to smaller manufacturers who can't afford weeks of expert setup per cell.

## How it works

NEURA builds robots with senses on board — 3D vision, force-torque feedback, plus voice and gesture interfaces — fused by AI so the machine perceives people and objects and reacts in real time. Its MAiRA cobot works cage-free next to humans; the 4NE-1 humanoid extends the same approach to whole-body tasks; and the Neuraverse platform supplies the operating system plus a marketplace where robot skills can be shared and sold.

## Pain points

Long integration queues for every new robot cell, fenced automation that can't flex with product changeovers, and a shortage of qualified integrators — exactly the bottlenecks a sensor-rich, redeployable robot plus a skills marketplace is designed to remove.

## Business model

Hardware sales across cobots, mobile manipulators, and humanoids, layered with Neuraverse platform software and skills-marketplace revenue. Industrial partnerships — Kawasaki Robotics, Omron Robotics and Safety Technologies, Delta Electronics — plus the 2025 GFT software-platform deal extend both distribution and development capacity.

## Challenges

Converting a claimed €1B order book into delivered, reliable robots; showing the 4NE-1 humanoid is market-ready rather than demo-ready; and holding a European manufacturing base while US and Chinese rivals outspend on humanoids and foundation models for robotics.
