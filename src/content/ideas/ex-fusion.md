## The problem

Japan has almost no domestic energy resources, and while laser fusion is the only fusion method to have experimentally achieved net energy gain, it remains a single-shot laboratory trick: nobody has strung reactions together continuously, so there is no reactor to commercialise.

## How it works

EX-Fusion, founded in 2021 and led by CEO Kazuki Matsuo, commercialises the LIFT fast-ignition design from Osaka University's Institute of Laser Science. Fuel targets are accelerated to 100 m/s and injected into the reactor; lasers from all directions implode the target, and a final laser pulse ignites fusion. A blanket absorbs the neutron energy, heats water, and drives a turbine. The engineering milestone that matters is cadence: repeating the cycle 10 times per second, steadily, like an engine.

## Pain points

Single-shot physics with no path to continuous operation; no commercial supply chain for rapid target injection and tracking; and Japanese industry's need for a domestic, carbon-free baseload technology it can own.

## Business model

Power plants are the endgame, but the enabling technologies pay along the way: high-power lasers, optical control, and target systems that sell into processing, medical, and space applications while the reactor matures.

## Challenges

Ten-hertz continuous fusion is still a goal, not a result; magnetic-confinement rivals are better funded; each step toward a demonstration reactor needs bigger cheques from a conservative investor base; and even the interim target — one hour of continuous neutron generation by fiscal 2027 — is hard.
