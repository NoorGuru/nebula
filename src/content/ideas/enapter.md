## The problem

Green hydrogen is hostage to megaprojects: conventional electrolysers demand huge plants, stable grids, and rare iridium — pricing out the remote farms, islands, refuelling points, and small industries that could use clean fuel today.

## How it works

Founded in 2017 by Sebastian-Justus Schmidt with Vaitea Cowan, Enapter built its stack around a patented anion exchange membrane that needs no iridium. The modules are small, standardised, and mass-produced at its Saerbeck campus, then stacked like building blocks: single units for off-grid sites, multicore systems for industry, and the 2026 Stack 250 generation (~100 kg of hydrogen per day per stack) scaling to 100 MW-plus plants — including hybrids with alkaline electrolysis.

## Pain points

Iridium dependence in PEM machines; multi-year bespoke plant projects; and every location where hydrogen is needed but fuel trucks cannot go — long-endurance drones, remote telecom, island grids.

## Business model

Sell the boxes at every scale: thousands of small AEM units to 360+ customers in 55+ countries, plus megawatt-class systems and 100 MW plant configurations for industrial customers decarbonising steel, chemicals, and fuels.

## Challenges

PEM and alkaline incumbents fight back on price at scale; the hydrogen economy is ramping slower than forecasts; margins on hardware are thin until volumes explode; and pilots — drones, refuelling demos — must convert into repeat multi-megawatt orders.

## Latest — Sep 2026

Received a ~€0.5 million pilot order from a US drone manufacturer for 4.1-series AEM electrolysers (delivery Q4 2026): the modules slot into mobile all-terrain refuelling systems that make hydrogen on-site from water and solar power — no cylinders, no fuel logistics — giving hydrogen drones far longer missions than batteries, and opening a scalable mobile-refuelling market.
